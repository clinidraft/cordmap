The level of a spinal cord injury decides what still works.

Messages between the brain and body travel down the spinal cord. After an injury, the body below that point stops getting them. Pick a level on the spine to see what that means for breathing, hands, walking, bladder and blood pressure.

    How much is affected: the ASIA grades

    The level says where the injury is. The ASIA Impairment Scale (AIS) says how complete it is, from A (complete) to E (normal). The key test is whether anything works in the lowest part of the cord, around the anus (segments S4–S5).

    1. A

      Complete

      No sensation or movement at all in S4–S5, the lowest sacral segments.

    2. B

      Sensory incomplete

      Some sensation is kept below the injury, including at S4–S5, but no movement more than three levels below the motor level.

    3. C

      Motor incomplete

      Some movement is kept below the injury, but fewer than half of the key muscles below the level can move against gravity.

    4. D

      Motor incomplete

      Movement is kept below the injury, and at least half of the key muscles below the level can move against gravity.

    5. E

      Normal

      Sensation and movement are normal in someone who previously had deficits.

    Incomplete injuries follow patterns

    When only part of the cord is damaged, the effects depend on which pathways are hit. Three pathways matter most, and they cross to the other side at different points, which is why some patterns affect each side differently.

    Injuries at the very bottom of the cord (conus medullaris) or to the nerve roots below it (cauda equina) are different again: they mainly affect the legs, bladder, bowel and sexual function, often with floppy rather than stiff muscles.

    • Key muscles, sensation points and the AIS grades follow the International Standards for Neurological Classification of Spinal Cord Injury (ISNCSCI), published by the American Spinal Injury Association.
    • Typical function by level is based on the Consortium for Spinal Cord Medicine's guidance on expected outcomes after complete injury (Paralyzed Veterans of America), written here in plain English.
